## 3. Boundary-source taxonomy

`FORM_BOUNDARY`:
grammar / derivation / syntax / well-formedness.

`CONSEQUENCE_BOUNDARY`:
tests, outcomes, anchors, external or formal consequences.

`VIABILITY_BOUNDARY`:
safety, collapse prevention, justitia-like shield, learner/population
protection.

`RULE_GENERATED_BOUNDARY`:
status or boundary follows from explicit rules without claim-specific human
field assignment.

`HUMAN_AUTHORED_BOUNDARY`:
boundary depends on manually supplied fields, assumptions, scopes, outcomes,
extension paths, or case design.

`POPULATION_BOUNDARY`:
stabilized usage / population agreement; never truth by itself.

`UNKNOWN_OR_MIXED_BOUNDARY`:
source unclear or inseparable mixture.

## 4. Boundary law audit

The boundary law is preserved:

```text
Never call a protective boundary a truth boundary.
Never call a truth boundary safe.
Never call a grammar boundary semantic.
Never call a human-authored boundary derived.
Never call a toy boundary world-like without a transfer gate.
Never call a rule-generated boundary meaningful without consequence pressure.
```

No S0-S2 claim survives B0 as stronger than its boundary source allows. The
major downgrade is that S2 is not a boundary generator; it is accounting and
replay over authored toy fields.

## 9. Truth / viability / rule trilemma

Truth boundary:
can support consequence contact, but in S2 exists only as toy test/outcome
tokens. External truth contact remains required for non-toy strength.

Viability boundary:
can shield the learner/population from collapse and proxy failure, but is bias,
not truth.

Rule-generated boundary:
can produce deterministic replay over finite fields, but is not meaningful
without consequence pressure.

Human-authored boundary:
can make finite toy cases auditable, but cannot be called derived evidence.
